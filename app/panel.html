Slices: every frequency at once

One small panadapter for each frequency you are measuring, side by side. With the Hermes Lite 2 they are all live together. The shaded stripe is the part that is measured. Shown automatically while logging; press Preview to check your antenna and gain before a drive.

Panadapter: see and hear the band

A live picture of a slice of the radio spectrum, like the display on a ham SDR. Click a signal to listen to it. Use it to find a quiet channel to measure, or to hunt for a noise source. The radio can do only one job at a time, so the full panadapter closes when you start logging. While logging you can still watch one of your frequencies (picture only, no sound), which is handy for spotting noise.


Logging is using the radio, so you can see this band but not tune or listen. The picture shows 2 MHz around your frequency (the red line). Stop logging, then press Start panadapter, to tune and listen.

Click a signal to tune. Drag the picture left or right (or swipe sideways on a trackpad) to move the view. The wheel, or the Up/Down keys in the Tuned box, change frequency by one step.



Attenuation reduces how strongly the radio amplifies, so strong signals do not overload it. The two boxes are the same settings used for recording (also under More settings in box 1), so what you see is what gets recorded. Raise the numbers if the picture fills with false signals or is crowded by strong stations; lower them to hear weak signals. Automatic gain is never used while recording; recording always uses these fixed numbers. If you change attenuation while logging, later dots are no longer directly comparable with earlier ones.

Audio recordings

Recordings made with the ● Record audio button in the panadapter. They are kept in the Recordings folder inside your RF Noise Map folder. They are small files (about 29 MB per hour, 8,000 samples a second) in the universal WAV format, so they play on any computer or phone and are fine for voice and CW.
